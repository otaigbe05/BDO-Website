**The short answer:** most of the winter tire rush is decided on Google before anyone calls you. When the first cold week arrives, drivers search, compare the shops that come up, and call or book whoever makes it easiest. Get your Google profile right, put a clear tire-swap page on your website, make booking possible without a phone call, and you will catch more of the rush with less chaos at the counter.

This guide is for owners of independent auto shops in Ontario. It takes about an hour to work through, and almost all of it is free.

## Why the rush comes all at once

Winter tires are not required in Ontario (Quebec is the only province where they are law), but most Ontario drivers use them anyway. The Tire and Rubber Association of Canada (TRAC) reports that **77 per cent of Ontario drivers used winter tires in 2025**, up from 74 per cent in 2024, and 80 per cent across Canada.

The timing is driven by temperature, not snow. TRAC's 2025 report says that **at 7 degrees Celsius or lower, winter tires outperform all-season and summer tires**, which start to harden and lose grip. So when daytime temperatures drop to around 7 degrees, a large share of your customers want the same job in the same few weeks.

There is also money on the table for them. Since January 1, 2016, Ontario insurers have had to reduce premiums for drivers with four winter tires installed. That is a fair, factual line to use when a customer is on the fence.

## 1. Fix your Google Business Profile first

Google says local results are based on three things: **relevance, distance and popularity**. You cannot change your distance from the driver, but you can make your profile complete and current, which is what relevance is about. These steps are all in your Business Profile, on Google Search or Maps.

- **Hours:** check your regular hours. If you open early or stay late during the rush, or open on a Saturday you normally don't, add them as special hours so Google does not send people to a closed door.
- **Services:** under Edit services, add the tire work you actually do, such as tire changes, seasonal tire swaps and tire storage. If your service is not in Google's list, choose Add custom service. Add a price or a short description where it helps.
- **Booking link:** in the Booking section, add the link to the page where customers pick a time. Use the booking page itself, not your home page.
- **A post:** add an Update or Offer post, for example "Winter tire swaps: book your spot", with a button to book. Posts are a simple way to show you are open and taking appointments right now.
- **Photos:** add a few recent, real photos: the bays, the tire rack, your sign. A profile with current photos looks like a shop that is open and busy, because it is.

## 2. Put a tire-swap page on your website

When someone finds you, the next question is "how much, how long, and can I book?". If your website does not answer it, they call, and in the rush the phone is your bottleneck. A short page or section for tire swaps answers it for them.

| What to include | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What the job is: a swap on rims, or mounting and balancing on the same rims | Customers often do not know the difference, and the price depends on it |
| Your price, or "from" a price | People compare; a missing price sends them to the next shop |
| How long it takes, and whether they can wait | Helps them plan the morning |
| Tire storage, if you offer it, and the price | Often the deciding factor for people with no garage space |
| What to bring: the tires, the wheel locking key | Saves time at the counter |
| A Book button and your hours | The whole point of the page |

Only list what you actually offer, at the price you actually charge. A page that is short and accurate beats a long one that is out of date.

## 3. Make booking the easy part

In a normal week, phone bookings work. In the rush, they do not: you are under a car while the phone rings, and the customer who cannot get through books somewhere else.

Online booking fixes the bottleneck. Customers pick a free slot at any hour, including the evening when they finally remember. A confirmation goes out straight away, and a reminder the day before cuts down on empty bays. You see the day fill up on your phone instead of on a notepad by the till.

If you already take online bookings, check two things before the rush: that your slots match the time a swap really takes in your shop, and that the booking link on Google and on your website goes to the right page.

## 4. Ask for reviews while the job is fresh

Google's own advice for local ranking includes getting reviews and replying to them. The rush is the best time of year for this, because you are seeing more customers in a few weeks than in most months.

Keep it simple and honest: when a customer picks up their car, ask if they would mind leaving a review, and send them the link. Reply to the reviews you get, good or bad, in a sentence or two. Never offer anything in exchange for a review, and never write reviews yourself.

## 5. Plan the spring swap now

Everyone who comes in for winter tires will need the swap back in spring. Keep a list of who came in, with their vehicle and a way to reach them, and send them a reminder when the weather warms up. It is the easiest booking you will get all year, because they already trust you.

## A simple timeline

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Before it gets cold | Fix your Google profile, add the tire-swap page, set up or check online booking |
| The first cold week | Post an update on Google, answer calls and messages fast, keep booking open |
| During the rush | Ask every happy customer for a review; reply to reviews |
| When the rush ends | Save the list of everyone who swapped, for the spring reminder |
